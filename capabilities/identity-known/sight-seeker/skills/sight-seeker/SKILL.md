# Sight Seeker

## Workflow

1. Choose ground or sky. On the ground record skyline shape, terrain, architecture and visible signs. In a sky view record the pattern relative to the horizon and ask for approximate region, date, local time and viewing direction when necessary. Work from a written description if the photo is unavailable.
2. Compare plausible candidates by at least one discriminating clue. Distinguish common shapes and silhouettes from a uniquely identifying feature. Light pollution, rotation, cropped frames and reflections can change the sky appearance. If the evidence is inadequate, stop at a broader description.
3. If verification is requested, suggest a public map or sky chart for the supplied place and time. Claim verification only when a reference was actually consulted and matches the evidence. Distinguish historical or cultural lore from the observations. Do not help pinpoint a private home or track a person from an image.
4. Return a caption or textual overlay key, such as upper-left tower or three stars above the tree line. Describe observed positions and uncertainty. Without a rendering or mapping tool, provide text rather than promising an annotated or geotagged file.

## Deliverable

Give the candidate or candidate group, evidence, unresolved clue and a copyable caption. Use only approximate location for sensitive subjects.

Verify that a point described as a confirmed landmark or sky object has a supporting discriminating clue and an independent match. Otherwise label it a candidate.

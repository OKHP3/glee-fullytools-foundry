# Maker Matcher

## Workflow

1. Ask whether the person wants the craft name, a material or technique, or a maker lead. Record visible joins, tool marks, repeated edges and surface texture. Describe what was actually observed separately from information provided by the seller or a label.
2. Name likely craft families, then distinguish closer techniques using evidence. Knots differ from interlaced crossings, for example. Offer a plain-language definition and one detail that could separate the alternatives. Suggest additional views only when they are safe to obtain.
3. For a maker request, use a mark or signature exactly as transcribed, dated provenance, or a maker directory supplied or actually consulted. Explain what would confirm a match. Similar style does not establish authorship, identity, origin or monetary value. Offer search terms if no directory is available.
4. Prepare a craft card or a short maker inquiry with neutral tags. If the object could involve blades, solvents, unknown powders or other hazards, limit the inquiry to available observations and manufacturer markings. Do not advise testing an unknown material by burning, tasting or touching it.

## Deliverable

Return craft family, candidate technique or maker lead, evidence, uncertainty and one safe way to learn more. Offer a short definition before specialist terminology.

Verify which construction detail supports each proposed technique and which evidence, if any, supports naming an individual maker.

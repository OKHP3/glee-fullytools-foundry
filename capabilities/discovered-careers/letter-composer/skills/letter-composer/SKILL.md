# Letter Composer

## Scope

Ask for the role, posting or role summary, truthful experience examples and any tone or length constraints. If the person supplies a draft, preserve their voice and any passages they ask to keep. When the organization or hiring contact is unknown, use an appropriate general greeting.

## Workflow

1. Pick one or two real needs from the posting. For each, select an example showing what the person did, why it mattered and the documented result. Keep the difference between an individual contribution and a team's outcome visible.
2. Open by naming the role and a specific reason for interest grounded in supplied material. Build the body around the examples. Close with a concise statement of interest and a practical next step. Avoid invented company news, personal connections and generic superlatives.
3. Edit for requested tone, length and plain reading order. Read the letter on its own and compare names, facts and metrics with the evidence. Keep questions or factual caveats outside the copyable letter.
4. If a posting is missing, deliver a clearly labeled general-interest draft or ask for a short role description. If an example is missing, provide a fill-in line and explain what evidence belongs there. Return text ready for the person's review and submission.

## Verify the result

Check the letter against the applicant’s source facts and the exact wording of any length or tone constraint. Read the copyable letter without its notes to catch accidental placeholders.

## Sources and limits

The original repository documents an early scaffold. This procedure implements the catalog's requested letter workflow. Delivery, file export and recruiter responses depend on other systems.

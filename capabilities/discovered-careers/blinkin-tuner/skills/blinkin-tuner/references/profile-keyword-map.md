# Profile keyword map

Use this worksheet to decide which target terms can appear in someone's LinkedIn copy. A hiring manager may treat even a short headline as a factual claim.

| Evidence label | What qualifies | Suitable placement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demonstrated | The person performed the work and can describe an example. | Headline, About or Experience as appropriate. |
| Learning | The person describes active study or practice, with its current scope. | About, explicitly framed as learning. A course exercise can appear as a clearly labeled project. |
| Unverified | The posting mentions the term, but the person supplies no example or study. | Keep in private editing notes as a question. |

## Build the profile copy

- **Headline**: lead with demonstrated work and a useful specialty. State a target as an interest if naming it could imply an appointment the person has never held.
- **About**: use the person's voice. Give a compact work example, then describe the problems or opportunities they want.
- **Experience**: retain the official role heading. Show the action and its scope. Attribute team outcomes accurately and use only measures the person supplied.
- **Keyword notes**: record the target term, evidence label, supporting fact and recommended placement. Keep these outside the public copy.

Example: someone who counted a store's stock weekly can support “inventory counts.” Someone practicing spreadsheet lookups can say they are learning that technique. A vacancy's request for demand forecasting alone establishes no forecasting experience.

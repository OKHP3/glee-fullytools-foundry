# bLinkIn Tuner

## Scope

Ask for the sections the person wants to change, the audience or target role, examples of their work and terms from representative opportunities. Pasted text is sufficient. For a profile link you cannot access, ask for the relevant section and avoid claiming to have opened it.

## Workflow

1. Identify the reader's likely question and the person's strongest proof. Compare the current headline with the role language. Classify each proposed term as supported by experience, a stated interest or unsupported.
2. Draft a headline that communicates role or discipline, specific work and a differentiator. Write an About section in the person's voice with a clear focus, one or two concrete examples and the kinds of opportunities they welcome.
3. Edit selected experience entries for scope, action and result. Retain formal job titles and dates. Distinguish team achievements from individual deliverables. Replace confidential project or client details with approved general terms.
4. Return labeled copy blocks for the requested sections, followed by the terms used and their evidence. If evidence is thin, ask for one observable example and offer a usable provisional line. The person chooses what to paste or publish.

## Edit the sections a reader will see

Start with the target reader and one posting or a short list of terms the person wants to be found for. Compare those terms with the supplied profile. Highlight one useful term the current text hides and one vague phrase the evidence can replace. Use the placement rules in `references/profile-keyword-map.md` when available. Someone who has studied a tool can describe the study with a qualifier. Someone who has only seen its name in a vacancy has no basis for a public skills claim.

Deliver the requested sections as independent copy blocks. Make the headline meaningful when viewed alone. Open About with what the person does and why a reader would care, then use one or two grounded examples. Put an individual's action and its context under the authentic Experience title. When the person has no profile yet, build these blocks from their work examples and target audience.

For instance, “organized weekend volunteer shifts” supports the search term “volunteer scheduling.” Calling the person a manager would require evidence of their actual responsibilities. Suggest a supported term in the copy and keep the reasoning, unanswered questions and alternate keywords in private editing notes.

## Apply a correction across the profile

Search every proposed section for a corrected term or figure. Revise ownership, scope and audience everywhere they appear. Preserve a supplied title as written and respect any instruction to leave an entire section untouched. If the user sets a character limit, count spaces and punctuation, then return a paste-ready version inside the limit. Give evidence labels separately when requested. The person can paste the result into their profile.

## Verify the result

Check that the headline, About section and role descriptions agree on seniority, duties and results. Remove keywords that the person could not support with the supplied examples.

## Sources and limits

The project guide and function catalog describe LinkedIn profile revision. The original hosted project listed these features as planned. This portable workflow produces copy for review. Search ranking and changes to a live profile require evidence from the platform.
